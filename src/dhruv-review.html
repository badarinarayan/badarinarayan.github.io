Dhruv

Love Story of an Alchemist  ·  by Karan Verma

★★★★★

Dhruv is one of those rare novels that refuses to leave you even after you've finished reading it.

While reading it, I often forgot I was holding a book. When I recall its scenes now, they feel less like pages from a novel and more like memories from a beautifully made film or a stage play. The characters are vividly etched, each serving a purpose in the story. Even their names seem thoughtfully chosen.

As the story unfolds, everyone predicts that Dhruv will one day shine like a star. But before that happens, every chapter throws him into the fire, testing him the way gold is purified. I kept wondering, what destiny awaits someone who is both selfless and ambitious?

Even his lady love is no exception. She suffers too. Every time life seems ready to reward them, something falls apart. One by one, he loses almost everyone dear to him. Just when I began to wonder whether life would ever smile upon him, he finally starts to shine—like the Pole Star itself, Dhruva Nakshatra.

What I particularly appreciated is that the novel doesn't carry the exaggerated touch of mainstream Hindi cinema. That's refreshing. Many Indian novels feel as though they are written as movie scripts, but Karan Verma avoids that trap. Yet, the storytelling remains deeply cinematic because of how naturally the characters evolve. Their growth, maturity, and emotional journeys feel earned.

The novel also reminds us that the success we imagine is rarely the success we eventually achieve.

I was reminded of a conversation shared by a Kannada actor, Ramesh Arvind. At an airport, a young woman told him she dreamed of becoming an actor, without recognizing who he was. What he has already achieved is still a dream for her. He reflected that while many people dream of the success he had achieved, he himself had never imagined that future during the phase of life when he was simply pursuing engineering, like countless others in South India.

Dhruv's journey is similar. His success is not what he dreamed of — but it is success nonetheless.

The story begins with his birth coinciding with his mother's death. Raised by a single parent who, despite love and sacrifice, becomes an obstacle to his ambitions, Dhruv learns that life rarely unfolds according to our plans. He finds love, but not in the way he imagined. Every major event in the novel quietly dismantles our comfortable assumptions about how life is "supposed" to work.

People often say, life is beautiful. Perhaps it is, but mostly to the onlooker. For the person actually living it, life is neither constantly beautiful nor constantly painful. It is simply being experienced, one day at a time, and in that process, we grow. It is the observer who labels events as good, bad, or ugly.

That, to me, is the heart of this novel.

Don't romanticize life. Live it. Experience it. Don't become a spectator in your own story.

Make the decisions that matter, even when they offer no guarantee of success or happiness. Do the right thing simply because it is the right thing to do.

Thank you, Karan Verma, for writing such a thoughtful and moving novel. I am yet to read Jack & Master, and I sincerely hope this is only one among many more to come.

Badarinarayan Joshi

Engineer · Writer · Builder
